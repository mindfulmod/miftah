# Letter Garden Picture-Book World Direction

## Experiential vision

Letter Garden is a living illustrated world where Arabic sounds wake sleeping
places. The child travels with a chosen pet through gardens, orchards, streams,
and courtyards. There is no written plot and no narrator explaining the
adventure. Each learned sound changes the world in a small, permanent way. A bud
opens, a bridge grows, a lantern lights, a bird returns, or a path continues.
The child understands the journey because they can see what their learning has
made possible.

Primary emotion: wonder.

Supporting emotions: warmth and capability.

## The new visual metaphor

The app is not a dashboard over a garden. It is a playable picture book.

- Navigation is an illustrated endpaper map.
- A learning world is a double-page spread.
- Opening a lesson is a visual page turn or journey into a detail.
- A lesson is an intimate close-up inside the same location.
- Completion reveals a changed version of the spread.
- Collections live in an illustrated field journal.
- Pet customization lives in a dressing nook inside the world.
- Adult controls live behind a separate gate and never share the child-facing
  visual language.

## World structure

### The travelling view

The main screen is one continuous panorama, not a chain of identical level
buttons. The path bends behind foreground plants and passes through large,
recognizable landmarks. The pet stands near the next place and looks toward it.
A golden trail of light appears only after the child pauses.

Completed places are visibly alive. Future places are present but quiet,
partially concealed, or asleep. Nothing reads as a wall of locks.

### Chapter rhythm

Each chapter follows five visual beats:

1. **Arrive:** wide view, quiet ambience, one curious change.
2. **Notice:** pet gaze and local motion identify the destination.
3. **Enter:** foreground closes like pages and reveals a close-up.
4. **Learn:** one sound, one clear interaction, generous visual quiet.
5. **Transform:** the sound changes the location and the camera returns wide.

### Suggested first chapter

The Dawn Orchard is the calibration chapter. A dry branch arches over a shallow
stream. Each completed sound grows a leaf, fruit, stepping stone, or visiting
bird. The chapter begins sparse and ends lush, so progression is visible without
numbers.

## Wordless interaction grammar

### What replaces written instructions

- The pet looks at the target.
- The target responds with one restrained movement.
- A sound plays.
- A translucent touch echo demonstrates the gesture once if the child waits.
- The first contextual cue waits three seconds, runs for no more than 1.15
  seconds, and stops permanently for that task as soon as the child interacts.
  It may preview movement but must never complete or grade the action.
- The rest of the composition becomes quieter until the child acts.

### What replaces English headings

Nothing. Screen identity comes from place, scale, and composition.

Arabic letters may be visible because they are the learning object. Arabic
instructional sentences should also be avoided in the early child flow. The
experience should work for a child who cannot read any language yet.

### What replaces progress bars

- A growing branch for the current chapter.
- Footprints or fireflies showing recent movement.
- A ring of seeds around the next landmark.
- The panorama itself becoming more complete.

### What replaces level nodes

Unique landmarks with different silhouettes and behaviors: a folded leaf, a
sleeping flower, a tiny waterwheel, a birdhouse, a lantern arch, a stepping
stone, or a hollow tree.

### What replaces generic cards

Learning objects exist in the scene. A letter can appear in the veins of a leaf,
on the face of a smooth stone, in reflected water, or inside a seed pod. Choice
objects use the same material family and are arranged as part of the close-up.

## Art language

### Medium

Recommended direction: layered gouache and cut paper with restrained colored
pencil detail.

Why:

- Gouache supports rich color with matte, child-friendly warmth.
- Cut-paper layers create readable depth and natural page-turn transitions.
- Pencil detail rewards close looking without introducing photorealism.
- Slight edge variation makes repeated assets feel authored.

The world should feel tactile, but not like a literal scrapbook. Paper is the
construction logic, not a decorative filter pasted onto every object.

### Shape language

- Landscapes: broad organic sweeps and overlapping paper planes.
- Learning objects: simple, iconic silhouettes with one irregular detail.
- Architecture: soft arches, courtyards, screens, and patterned shade.
- Plants: species-specific leaf and flower shapes, not repeated blob foliage.
- Characters: approved chunky proportions, warm faces, and clear gesture arcs.
- Contrast accent: small star, seed, or teardrop shapes for magic and sound.

### Cultural grounding

The world should feel connected to Arabic learning through botanical gardens,
courtyard spatial rhythms, regional plants, geometric shade patterns, woven
textiles, illuminated-manuscript color relationships, and calligraphic movement.

Avoid generic desert-palace fantasy, excessive domes, ornamental overload,
theme-park exoticism, or assigning invented sacred meaning to decorative motifs.
Specific cultural references require sourced visual research and review.

### Palette script

The palette changes by chapter rather than applying one brand palette to every
screen.

- Dawn Orchard: apricot light, pistachio leaves, soft turquoise water, fig brown.
- Singing Courtyard: chalk limestone, lapis shade, saffron cloth, jasmine white.
- Reed River: blue-green water, clay banks, silver reeds, coral insects.
- Moon Garden: indigo sky, muted jade, lavender shadow, warm lantern gold.

Reserved roles across every chapter:

- Warm golden light: next discovery and successful learning.
- Deep fig brown: important contours and character eyes.
- Soft cream: visual quiet and touch affordances.
- Coral: brief celebration accent only.

### Light and material

- One soft light direction per spread.
- Matte surfaces with broad value changes, not glossy vector highlights.
- Contact shadows are colored and soft.
- Magic illuminates nearby paper edges rather than creating a generic neon glow.
- Textures are low-frequency and tied to material: paper fiber, dry brush,
  pencil, woven cloth, or stone grain.

### Calibrated rendering hierarchy

- Distant landscape contour: 4 to 5 CSS-pixel equivalent, fig brown at roughly
  70 to 80 percent visual strength.
- Landmark and companion contour: 5 to 6 CSS-pixel equivalent, full fig brown.
- Current letter and answer contour: 6 to 7 CSS-pixel equivalent, the darkest
  edge in the spread.
- Paint grain remains under 10 percent opacity and is applied by material layer,
  never as high-frequency noise on glyphs, eyes, or interaction icons.
- Dry-brush scumbles use broad 12 to 34 pixel strokes and remain below 22
  percent opacity.
- Environmental cast shadows use 5 to 6 pixels of vertical displacement and 5
  pixels of softness. Character contact shadows sit directly beneath the feet
  and never imply that the pet is floating.
- Characters remain one half-step cleaner than the environment, but share its
  outline hue, light direction, matte finish, and shadow color.

## Composition system

Every spread uses three attention layers:

1. **Focal action:** one dominant learning object or destination.
2. **Story support:** pet, environmental cause, and a clear route between them.
3. **Discovery layer:** two to four optional details near borders or landmarks.

At least 35 percent of a learning spread remains visually quiet. Only the focal
action may use strong motion, strongest value contrast, and the reserved golden
light at the same time.

Scale should alternate:

- Wide panorama for arrival and transformed-world payoff.
- Medium character view for anticipation and companionship.
- Close detail for listening, tracing, matching, or assembling.

### Lesson-family contract

Every activity keeps the same close control, sound control, companion position,
success response, and world-growth transition. Only the learning object and its
response area change.

- Listening is audio-first. The large printed target is withheld and the sound
  bloom becomes the single focal object before the child chooses.
- Visual discrimination keeps the target visible and presents related forms on
  botanical objects. Material detail always sits behind the glyph and never
  crosses its readable silhouette.
- Assembly presents a small completed reference and one large incomplete form.
  Movable dots or pieces use the gold interaction color, while empty sockets use
  a quiet dashed fig outline.
- Assembly introduces no more than two movable pieces in the first chapter.
- Wrong responses settle locally and replay the sound. They do not shake the
  screen, remove progress, or trigger a negative facial expression.
- No lesson depends on written instructions, including Arabic instructions.

## Motion language

- Page turn: 520 to 700 ms, layered foreground overlap, no flat crossfade.
- Pet gaze cue: 700 ms, then stillness.
- Learning-object cue: one 900 ms movement, repeated once only after waiting.
- Ambient loops: 5 to 18 seconds, under 8 px at phone scale.
- Correct response: environment reacts within 180 ms, transformation completes
  within 900 ms.
- Wrong response: object leans or settles, pet becomes curious, and the sound is
  offered again. No red flash, sad face, buzzer, or screen shake.
- Celebration: one environmental payoff plus a short particle accent. Never a
  full-screen particle curtain by default.
- Returning to the panorama after a completed lesson blooms the learned
  landmark and wakes the next landmark once. The companion celebrates during
  the same 1.4-second window; after that, only the normal current-place pulse
  continues.

Reduced motion replaces travel and page turns with two illustrated still states
and a short opacity transition. Learning state never depends on motion alone.

## Re-imagining every existing Letter Garden surface

| Existing surface | Picture-book replacement |
| --- | --- |
| Hatch | A sleeping seed or nest discovered in the opening endpaper; taps reveal signs of life before the companion appears. |
| Home map | A continuous illustrated panorama with unique landmarks, occluded paths, and visible world growth. |
| Meet a letter | The child follows the pet into a close-up where the sound emerges from a leaf, stone, water reflection, or crafted object. |
| Mini-games | Small visual events inside the location, each with a different physical metaphor instead of one generic game frame. |
| Stars screen | The location transforms immediately; optional stars become small collectible light-seeds inside the scene. |
| World party | A wide transformed spread with returning creatures, music, and the pet sharing the result. |
| Pet room | A cozy dressing nook with a mirror, hooks, shelf, fabric basket, and color objects; accessories are tried directly on the pet. |
| Sticker album | A field journal or cabinet of curiosities whose pages fill with found objects and creatures. |
| Daily streak | A sequence of sun and moon tokens, footprints, or watered seedlings, shown only when relevant. |
| Grown-up area | A separate library-door or gate transition using adult-readable Arabic or localized text outside the child flow. |
| Top HUD | Contextual object controls; persistent chrome is reduced to home, audio, and companion access when required. |

## Technical production direction

Use a hybrid asset system:

- Layered WebP spreads for painted background, midground, and foreground planes.
- SVG for Arabic glyphs, interaction symbols, fitted accessories, and precise
  state graphics.
- Existing pet rigs as the behavioral source, with a new material treatment only
  after the composition calibration passes.
- CSS transforms and opacity for page layers and small environmental motion.
- Canvas or DOM particles only for short, bounded celebration effects.

Do not rasterize text or core interaction symbols. Do not build the world from a
single flat background image if foreground occlusion, page turns, and visible
progression need independent control.

## Calibration kit before production

Build one vertical slice only:

1. Dawn Orchard panorama before learning.
2. One close-up letter lesson inside that location.
3. The same panorama after the world transformation.
4. Rafi in idle, guiding, curious, and celebrating states within the new
   material language.
5. One wordless home control, one audio control, and one environmental progress
   marker.

### Pass criteria

- A child can find the first action without reading or adult explanation.
- The lesson target is the first or second visual fixation.
- The world feels different after completion without checking a score.
- The scene reads at 390 by 844 without cropping the pet or target.
- Optional details reward exploration but do not receive the first tap.
- The pet and environment appear painted by the same hand.
- The spread remains clear with ambient motion disabled.
- No visible English appears anywhere in the child flow.

## Open creative decisions

1. Whether the cultural grounding should be subtly botanical and architectural,
   or more visibly inspired by illuminated manuscripts and regional craft.
2. Whether the material should lean softer and painterly or bolder and more
   graphic within the recommended gouache and cut-paper family.
3. Whether Blob remains the opening companion and animals unlock later, or the
   child chooses all four companions at the opening seed moment.

These should be resolved before the calibration kit is produced.
